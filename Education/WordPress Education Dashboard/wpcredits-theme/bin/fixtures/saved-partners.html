WordPress Credits Program

Fund real student contributions to WordPress

WordPress Credits is a program of the WordPress Foundation, a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization. Students at partner institutions contribute to the WordPress project for academic credit, guided by mentors and recognized on their WordPress.org profiles. Partner support funds the curriculum, mentorship, translation and tooling behind those placements.

What your support funds

The program has reached the size where curriculum, translation, tooling, and the work of bringing new institutions on board all need steady funding behind them. Partner contributions go into that work directly.

  • Curriculum and mentorship

    Course materials, mentor time, and the review work that keeps student contributions at project standard.

  • Translation and accessibility

    Bringing the program, and the work students produce, to languages and communities with thin coverage today.

  • Institutional onboarding

    Tooling, administration, and the support each new university or school needs to run a cohort well.

2027 is a pilot year

This is the first time we have offered partnership levels, and we are treating it as a test. We will run it through 2027, publish what we learn, and adjust or retire whatever does not work. If none of the levels below fits what your company wants to do, write to us and we will shape something that does.

Partnership levels

Five levels, from contributing mentor time through to funding a complete program. Recognition is consistent across all of them: partners are listed in the same format, with descriptions of the same length, in alphabetical order rather than by level.

Compare all levels →

  • Friend

    In-kind, through Five for the Future

    For companies sponsoring mentors through Five for the Future.

    • Company name on the partner page
    • Mention in the mentor feature
    • Acknowledgement in the annual partner thank you post

  • Supporter

    $5,000 a year

    Everything in Friend, plus:

    • Logo and description on the partner page
    • A badge for your own site
    • The annual program report

  • Backer

    $10,000 a year

    Everything in Supporter, plus:

    • Named for the part of the program you fund in program communications
    • A report covering that cost line
    • One self-written educational post about the sponsored product

  • Champion

    $15,000 a year

    Everything in Backer, plus:

    • A second self-written post with factual, value-neutral information about additional products
    • An invitation to the annual education partners session
    • A webinar or event co-hosted with the education program

  • Partner Funds a named program

    From $30,000 a year

    Everything in Champion, plus a complete program funded end to end, the kind we have not been able to build yet because we have always had to work within the budget available. Students translating WordPress into languages with thin coverage, students working on accessibility, students building sites for nonprofit organizations that could not otherwise commission them.

    • Named as program partner on the program you fund, with the exclusive role in that program
    • Named in launch communications
    • A report specific to that program

Products donated for student use

Separately from the levels above, partners at any level may offer free access to their products for students to use in the program, and the Foundation decides how those are shared. Donated products are offered at no cost to students, require no card details to use, and come with a way for students to export or move their work so nobody is locked in.

How student work is used

Student work always serves the public commons or a nonprofit beneficiary, never a partner's commercial product. All partners are listed in the same format, with descriptions of the same length, in alphabetical order rather than by level. These levels cover the WordPress Credits program only.

Founding partners

Automattic and Weglot

Automattic and Weglot have supported WordPress Credits since it was an idea on a page, sponsoring the people who designed and built the program and the mentors who guide students cohort after cohort. None of the numbers on this page would exist without them, and the levels above are built on what they made possible.
