# Sponsor guide

*Your Sponsor Dashboard, your offers and codes, what your company can see, and how posts are approved.*

Thank you for sponsoring the WordPress Credits Program. This guide covers the page your company has on the Education Hub, how offers and codes reach students and mentors, what the program shows you and what it keeps to itself, and how a post you write is reviewed.

## Signing in

Nobody registers on this site, and there is no sign-up form: the program makes every account for
the person it belongs to. That means two things on your first visit:

1. Go to the site's login page and use **Lost your password?** with the email address on your
   account. A reset link arrives at that address.
2. Set a password, and you are in - unless your account also asks for a code, which the next
   part covers.

If the reset email never arrives, the address on your account is probably not the one you are
checking. The login page cannot change it - ask whoever runs the program.

### Signing in with a code

Besides your password, your account may ask for a code. Whether it does is the program's choice,
and it is made for every account of your kind at once. When it does, there is nothing to set up
first: from your next sign-in, the code arrives by email. When it does not, signing in works as
described above, and a card on your page invites you to turn the code on for yourself.

After you enter your username and password, a second screen asks for the code. Which kind depends
on how your account is set up.

**An emailed code.** The screen says a verification code has been sent to the email address on your
account. The email comes from this site and has *Login confirmation code* in its subject line. It
holds a code of eight digits. Type it into **Verification Code** and press **Verify**. A code works
once, and for fifteen minutes. **Resend Code** sends a new one and the new one replaces the old, so
use the newest email.

**An authenticator app.** If you have set one up, the screen asks for the code from your app
instead: **Authentication Code**. Open the app, find this site in it, and type the six digits it
shows. The digits change every 30 seconds, so type the ones on the screen at that moment.

A wrong code does not lock you out for good, but the site makes you wait a moment before the next
try, and the wait grows with every wrong code, up to fifteen minutes. Wait it out, then type the
code again, carefully.

### Setting up an authenticator app

An app on your phone is quicker than waiting for an email, and it keeps working when your inbox
does not. The card at the top of your page starts it. Its heading is **Add a second step to your
sign-in** when the code is yours to choose, and **Your account asks for a code when you sign in**
when the program asks for one; its button, **Turn on two-factor authentication** or **Set up an
authenticator app**, opens your profile screen at its **Two-Factor Options**. Ignoring the card
costs nothing: signing in keeps working the way it does now.

If you already use emailed codes and you open that screen more than about ten minutes after you
signed in, it first asks you to confirm with **Revalidate now**, which takes one more code. Then
follow the steps on the screen: install an authenticator app on your phone (any will do, such as
Google Authenticator or Microsoft Authenticator), scan the QR code the screen shows with it, and
type in the code your app then displays. Once the app is set up the card stops appearing.

Before you leave that screen, generate the **recovery codes** (the card calls them backup codes)
and keep them somewhere other than your phone. Each works once, and the screen shows them only
once. They are how you get back in if you lose the phone.

### If you cannot get your code

- **The email does not come.** Look in your spam folder, then press **Resend Code**. The email goes
  to the address on your account, so if you no longer read that mailbox, an emailed code cannot
  reach you.
- **Your phone or your app is gone.** Under **Having Problems?** on the same screen, the site
  lists any other way your account has, for example **Send a code to your email**, or **Use a
  recovery code** if you saved some. Type one of your saved recovery codes where the screen asks
  for it, and cross that code off, because it will not work twice.
- **You have neither.** Without the emailed code, the app or a recovery code, the site cannot tell
  that it is you, and it lets nobody past that screen. Tell whoever runs the program that you are
  locked out, and give them the username you sign in with. This guide cannot fix it for you.

### Once you are signed in

Your page is drawn for whoever is signed in, so the same address shows each person their own
page, and no change to a link shows anybody else's. The
program's Administrators are the one exception: to help, they can open anyone's page, through a
switcher only they are shown. A company's people share one Sponsor Dashboard: everyone the program
attached to the company sees the same page.

## Your Sponsor Dashboard

Your company's page on the Education Hub is the Sponsor Dashboard, at /sponsor-dashboard/. You reach it after signing in with the account the program created for your company; every person the program attached to your company sees the same page. It opens with your company's name, logo and details, then **At a glance**, a row of tiles with the figures you read first, then your cards in four groups, **Your sponsorship**, **What you offer**, **Mentors** and **Your company**, in that order, and last, below the cards, the program's updates, its resources and your contact at the program.

A tile at zero is dimmed, not hidden. Press a tile and the page takes you to the card that holds it, opened. The event tile is there while your sponsorship includes events, which it does from Champion up until your term ends or the program pauses your sponsorship, and while an event of yours is still to take place; the report tile is there once your first yearly report is finished.

## Offers, codes and claims

An offer is something your company gives the program's participants: a license, a plan, a credit, a course seat, a discount. It is of one of two kinds. **A pool of one-time codes** is a list of codes you paste or upload, one per line or as a CSV; each code is handed to one person, once. **One code or link everyone uses** is a single code or checkout link that every eligible person sees.

**Creating an offer.** The *New offer* form sits on the left of the *Offers and codes* card. Give the offer a title, say what a person gets in a sentence or two, how to redeem it, and where to find more information. Choose the kind. For a pool, paste the codes or upload a .txt or .csv file; codes are stored encrypted and shown only to the person who claims one. Choose who else the offer is open to: current students always are; mentors and the program team are your choice. Set the number of codes at which the program should warn you that the pool is running low and, if the offer has a last day, that day. Press *Create offer*: the offer starts as a draft that nobody but you can see. Above the form, a line says that offers are separate from the tiers: as the program's partner page says, partners at any level may offer free access to their products, so your company may have any number of live offers, whatever its tier. Drafts are unlimited.

**The review.** An Administrator reads every offer before students and mentors see it, and a line above the *New offer* form says so. When a draft is ready, press *Send for review*: the offer is then *Waiting for review*, still seen by nobody but you, and *Take it back* makes it a draft again. An Administrator approves it, and it goes live, or returns it with a note, which reaches you by email and shows under the offer's state: change the offer, then send it for review again. Once an offer has been approved, a change to what students see of it, its title, what a person gets, how to redeem it, where to find more information and, for one code everyone uses, the code or link itself, is saved and waits for review too, while students and mentors go on seeing the offer as it was approved. The offer says so under its details, and *Discard the changes* drops what waits. An approved change takes the place of what students saw; a returned one stays under the offer's details with the note, until you change it again or discard it. Everything else takes effect at once, with no review: pausing, resuming and ending an offer, its last day, who it is open to, the number of codes the warning waits for, and voiding the codes nobody has claimed. While anything of yours waits, the card's line says how many offers and changes wait for review.

**Adding codes.** Codes you add to a draft, or to an offer waiting for review, go in at once and are read with the offer when it is reviewed. A draft the program made for you, such as your first offer, can be switched on with no review; once you add codes to it, it needs one like any other, and its *Switch on* becomes *Send for review*. Codes you add to an offer that has been approved, whether it is live, paused or dormant, wait for an Administrator's review, since a code can be a whole checkout link: *Add codes* says "Your codes were saved and wait for an Administrator's review. Until they are approved, nobody can claim them.", and the offer says how many codes you added wait, beside any change of yours that waits. Until they are approved, nobody is handed one, the counts of the offer, and of what students see as left, leave them out, and the warning that the pool is running low stays as it was. An approval puts them in the pool with any change that waits, as one decision. A return takes them out of the offer with its note, which reaches you by email and shows under the offer's details with how many codes were taken out: add them again, changed or not, to send them for review once more. *Discard the changes* takes them out too, at once. A code that waits is still in the offer for the repeat check, so it cannot be added twice, and it counts toward the 5,000 an offer holds. *Void unclaimed codes* leaves the codes that wait as they are, so you can void an old batch and add a new one in either order. Ending an offer takes the codes that wait for review on it out of the offer. Codes an Administrator adds for you go in at once. Offers that students could already see before the review began stay as they are.

**Your offers.** The offers you have sit on the right, under *Active offers*, each folded to its title, its state and a line of counts: for a pool, how many codes are available, claimed and void; for a shared code, how many people took it. Open one to edit it, to pause, resume or end it and, for a pool, to add codes or void the ones nobody has claimed. An offer that has ended, or whose last day has passed, moves under *Ended and expired offers*.

**States.** A **draft** is visible to nobody but you: send it for review when it is ready. An offer **waiting for review** is visible to nobody but you either, until an Administrator approves it. A **live** offer is shown to the people it is open to; the one live offer marked *primary* is the offer the program names as yours. A **paused** offer is hidden until you resume it, and its codes stay. A **dormant** offer is one the program held back while a tier capped live offers: it is hidden, its codes stay, the codes already claimed stay with the people who hold them, and you can end it but not resume it. No tier caps live offers now, so a dormant offer comes back by itself on the program's next daily run; one whose last day has passed stays as it is. An **ended** offer is over: nothing more can be claimed, it cannot be resumed, and the codes already claimed stay with the people who hold them.

**Claims.** A participant claims an offer from their own page: a student from the Student Report Card, a mentor from the Mentor Report Card, both in a section called Tools from our sponsors. For a pool, the program hands out the next available code, seals it to that person, and never shows it to anyone else, including you. When a pool falls below the number you set, the *Offers and codes* card says so and the program mails you once.

**What you see.** The *Usage* card counts claims by month and by offer: a chart of the last twelve months, a table with the totals, and a CSV of the same. Nobody is named there: the program keeps the list of who claimed which code, so that it can help a person whose code failed, and does not share it.

## What your company can and cannot see

The Sponsor Dashboard shows your company its own things: its profile, its people, its agreement, its offers, its usage counts, its interests, its posts and its mentors. It never shows another sponsor's page, and it never shows who a student or a mentor is beyond what that person chose to make public. Once the program turns Sponsor tiers on, the Partners page lists your company by name at every tier, in alphabetical order with every other partner and in the same format, adding your logo, your website and your description from Supporter up; your badge, from Supporter up, names only the program, your tier and the year, with no logo or other company detail of its own.

- **Students.** You see counts: how many students each of your mentors guides, how many people claimed an offer. You do not see names, addresses, reports, grades or the notes of a call. Your *Reports* card is the same: it names your mentors and counts, never a student.
- **Mentors.** You see the mentors your company sponsors by name and WordPress.org profile, with how many students each one guides, and the active mentors who asked to be listed as looking for a sponsor. You do not see a mentor's calendar, notes or feedback.
- **Claims.** You see numbers, by month and by offer. The program keeps the list of who claimed which code, so that it can help a person whose code failed, and does not share it with sponsors.
- **Events.** You see how many people joined your co-hosted event, on the *Events* card, never who: the names are for Administrators alone, and no mail your company receives carries one; the reminder the day before your event says how many have joined so far, and nothing more about them. The call link you give reaches the people who join and the program, and no list shows it to anybody else. Your *Reports* card is the same: the yearly report lists the events held in the term with how many joined, and no names. Once the program approves your event, the Partners page lists it under *Upcoming events* until it ends, for anybody to read: its title, your company's name linked to your website, its day and time in the site's time zone, its length and its description, with any address in it that starts with http://, https:// or mailto: taken out; never the call link, the places or who joined. Under the list, a visitor who is not signed in finds a *Sign in to join* button, since the people the event is for join it on their own pages; a student, mentor, institution contact or Administrator who is signed in finds a link to that page instead, such as *Join it on your Student Report Card*, and anybody else who is signed in, such as someone who signs in only for your company, finds neither. An address written without http://, https:// or mailto:, such as an email address on its own or one starting with www., stays in the description as you wrote it. The event leaves the page if the program pauses your sponsorship or your sponsorship stops including events.
- **Records.** The details you save on your profile card, your logo, the offer marked *primary* and what you send from *Offer more* are kept on this site, where Administrators read them.
- **The program's people.** Administrators see your page as you see it, and can act on it for you: create an account for a colleague, record your agreement as on file, review a post. The switcher above the page, *Viewing as sponsor*, is theirs alone.

What you write on the dashboard is kept under the program's privacy policy, linked from the bottom of every page. Ask your program contact if you want something removed.

## Posts, and how they are approved

From the Backer tier up, your company can write posts for the program's students, as the program's partner page says: a Backer one self-written educational post about the sponsored product, and a Champion or a Partner a second, with factual, value-neutral information about additional products. Posts are written in the site's editor. The *Posts* card on the Sponsor Dashboard has the *Write a post* button and lists every post your company wrote, with where each one stands.

**Writing.** A Backer has one post at a time, in review or published; a Champion or a Partner has two. Press *Write a post*, write in the editor, and submit the post for review. A post carries your company's byline and lands in your company's category, so readers know who wrote it. A post can carry images and links; it cannot carry code that runs on the page. When your tier includes no posts, or no more of them right now, the *Posts* card says so where *Write a post* would be, and a post submitted anyway stays a draft. A post the program took off the site because your tier no longer includes it is **dormant**: the newest posts go first, the card says why, and a dormant post returns by itself when an Administrator sets a tier that includes it. If you edit a dormant post, it becomes a new submission, which an Administrator reviews before it is published again.

**Review.** Every post is read by an Administrator before it is published. The Administrator can **publish** it, or **return** it with a note that says what to change; a returned post comes back to you with the note on it, and you can submit it again. A published post appears to students and mentors under *Guides from your company* on your offer's card in their Tools section, and in the site's Sponsors category, which only signed-in participants can read.

**Rules.** Posts are for the program's participants: useful to a student learning WordPress, honest about what your product does, free of pricing pressure. The program's licensing and trademark rules apply to what a post says and links to. A post that reads as an advertisement is returned.

If posting is switched off for your company, the *Posts* card says so; ask your program contact.
